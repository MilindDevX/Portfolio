## Project Overview

As the Project Lead and ETL Lead on a 6-member capstone, I engineered an end-to-end analytics pipeline on 43,824 hourly observations from the UCI Beijing PM2.5 dataset (2010–2014).

## The Process

- **Data Engineering**: Handled complex datetime synthesis, forward-fill imputation for 2,067 nulls, and feature engineering of AQI categories and seasonal markers.
- **Statistical Analysis**: Conducted 7 non-parametric statistical tests (Spearman, Kruskal-Wallis, Mann-Whitney), establishing wind speed as the strongest pollution reducer, and identifying seasonal alerts as statistically justified.
- **Visual Analytics**: Designed a comprehensive 6-KPI framework (Annual Mean, WHO Ratio, Hazardous Rate, AQI Distribution). Produced 11 EDA visualizations—including monthly time series and hourly heatmaps—culminating in a dual-view Tableau dashboard.

## Key Outcomes

- Revealed that Beijing’s PM2.5 exceeded WHO limits by 6–7x every year, with air classified unsafe for over 50% of all recorded hours.
- Formulated 3 data-driven operational recommendations: winter-priority coal emission curtailment, NW-wind "Clean Window" alerts, and calm-wind emergency industrial caps.
- Projected a 10–15% reduction in peak PM2.5 exposure based on proposed models.
